===============================================================
CALIBRATION  -  read before scoring anything
===============================================================
- Score the evidence, not the vocabulary. A CV is a sales document; most of it
  is written to sound like the top band.
- Claims with no outcome attached, tool and platform lists, certifications,
  course names, institution names, job titles and years of experience are
  BASELINE. They cannot score above 3 on any criterion, however senior they
  sound. This includes elite institutions and well-known product courses -
  they are not evidence of any criterion here.
- Generic professional phrasing - "fast-paced environment", "cross-functional
  collaboration", "stakeholder management", "data-driven decisions",
  "end-to-end ownership" with nothing named - is baseline. Score it 3, not 5.
- A number is only evidence if it is attached to something the candidate did.
  "Managed a 40-person platform" is baseline; "cut P1 incidents 40% in 6 months
  by introducing code review and on-call" is not.
- Seniority in the title is not seniority in the decision. Ask who made the call.
- Reserve 9-10 for evidence a sceptical reader could not argue with: named,
  specific and consequential.
- If two bands look defensible, take the lower one. Under-scoring is
  recoverable - the candidate is still visible below the line. Over-scoring
  puts the wrong person in front of the founder.

PRODUCT MANAGER
---------------------------------------------------------------

Criterion name: Operational Accountability in Logistics
Weight: 25%
What a strong candidate looks like: Has held a role where freight,
documentation, customs, carriers or warehouse execution was their personal
responsibility and failure had a same-day consequence - freight forwarding,
CHA, port or terminal services, 3PL, NVOCC, carrier or shipper operations.
Weak: logistics appears only as a customer, a vertical they sold into, or an
API they integrated against.
Score bands:
  0-2   No contact with logistics, freight, supply chain or trade operations
        anywhere in the record.
  3-4   Adjacency only - built or sold software for logistics customers,
        integrated a 3PL or courier API, worked at a logistics company in a
        function with no operational consequence, or names supply chain in a
        course, certification or interest line. Baseline exposure does not
        clear 4.
  5-6   An operations-adjacent role where logistics outcomes were one input
        among several - planning, analytics or account management touching
        shipments, but not accountable when something was stuck.
  7-8   At least a year in a role where freight, documents or carriers were
        their direct responsibility - CHA, freight forwarder desk, port or
        terminal services, NVOCC, 3PL exception management - with specific
        artefacts named (Bill of Lading, Shipping Bill, DO, HBL, berth window,
        EDI filing).
  9-10  Multiple years of that, plus at least one named incident they
        personally resolved under a live deadline - a customs hold, an
        inspection, a detention, a missed berth window.
Source: Rohan, Sunita, Aditya, Meghna, Lavanya (all Exceeds). Absent in
Vikram, Rahul, Preetham.

Criterion name: Ownership Without Cover
Weight: 20%
What a strong candidate looks like: Named as the sole or most senior owner of a
product area, process or account book, with the decision sitting with them.
Weak: consistently described as supporting or contributing inside a team where
someone more senior made the call.
Score bands:
  0-2   Every responsibility described as supporting, assisting or
        contributing. No scope they owned.
  3-4   Owned tasks or features inside a team where a senior person set
        direction. A title containing "lead" or "senior", with no evidence the
        call sat with them, stays here.
  5-6   Owned a defined area, but inside a function that had a head - they set
        the how, someone else set the what.
  7-8   Sole or most senior owner of an area for a sustained period - "sole
        PM", "no manager above", self-employed, or ran a cycle end to end -
        with at least one decision of theirs named.
  9-10  The above, plus evidence others deferred to their judgment: a review
        quote, a practice they set, a call the team adopted.
Source: Lavanya "sole PM"; Sunita self-employed; Aditya "no account manager
layer". Contrast Vikram "supported senior PMs", Preetham "part of the
12-engineer team".

Criterion name: Documented Reversal or Failure
Weight: 20%
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV itself names something that did not
work - a feature killed, a deal lost, an outage, a bug raised against their own
product - together with what they did next.
Score bands:
  0-2   Every bullet is a win. Nothing stopped, lost, rolled back or
        reconsidered anywhere.
  3-4   A problem is mentioned but it belongs to someone else - a legacy
        system, a vendor, a previous team. No exposure of their own.
  5-6   Names something that went wrong inside their scope and how it was
        fixed, without owning the decision that caused it.
  7-8   Names a decision or build of their own that was wrong or was killed,
        and what they did next - a feature killed on usage data, a deal lost,
        an outage they wrote up.
  9-10  The above, and the lesson became a practice other people now follow.
Source: Lavanya killed 2 features and wrote the outage post-mortem; Aditya's
documented lost deal. Absent in Vikram, Rahul, Preetham.

Criterion name: Built the Missing Thing
Weight: 20%
What a strong candidate looks like: Created a tool, document or process that
did not exist and that nobody assigned them, in an organisation that had no
owner for it - and other people were still using it afterwards.
Score bands:
  0-2   No artefact created. Work described entirely as executing what already
        existed.
  3-4   Improved, templated or documented something that already had an owner
        and a process. Certifications and tool lists count for nothing here.
  5-6   Built something new for their own use or their immediate team, with no
        evidence it outlived them.
  7-8   Built something that did not exist, unprompted, in a place that had no
        owner for it, and named adoption by other people.
  9-10  The above, and it became permanent or standard - still in use, adopted
        across teams, or absorbed into the product.
Source: Rohan's BoL module (weekend build, 30 users in a month, now core
product); Sunita's weekend workflow rebuild, retained permanently; Meghna's
onboarding framework now used by the whole CS team.

Criterion name: Absorbed Load Without Escalation
Weight: 15%
What a strong candidate looks like: Carried a surge, a departure or a crisis
without extra headcount or handing it upward.
Score bands:
  0-2   No evidence of operating under pressure at all.
  3-4   Describes a busy environment or high volume in general terms, with no
        incident named and no indication the load was theirs to hold.
  5-6   Handled an increase in scope, but with added support, headcount or an
        extended timeline.
  7-8   Carried a surge, a departure or a crisis at their own level without
        extra resource - covered a colleague's book, absorbed volume through
        process redesign, resolved a problem before it escalated.
  9-10  The above, sustained over months, with the outcome held - no churn, no
        slippage, no penalty.
Source: Meghna covered a departing CSM's 8 accounts for 3 months with no churn;
Rohan's 18-month volume surge with no added headcount.

SENIOR PRODUCT MANAGER

Criterion name: Ownership Without Cover
Weight: 30%
What a strong candidate looks like: Final decision-maker on a product area,
integration surface or business line for a sustained period, with no senior
peer to escalate the call to. The bar is not autonomy on tasks but authority on
direction.
Score bands:
  0-2   Supporting roles only. No scope owned.
  3-4   Owned features or projects while a head of product, a founder or a
        committee made the directional calls. Years of experience alone does
        not move this.
  5-6   Owned an area independently but inside a strategy someone else set, or
        for less than a year.
  7-8   Final decision-maker on a product area, integration surface or business
        line for a sustained period, with named decisions and named
        consequences they lived with.
  9-10  The above, plus they set how the function itself worked - standards,
        frameworks or decision rules that others then ran on.
Source: Sunita's five self-employed years scoping, vendor-managing and running
go-live alone; Lavanya as sole PM making calls engineering trusted immediately.

Criterion name: Operational Accountability in Logistics
Weight: 20%
What a strong candidate looks like: Personal accountability inside a logistics
operation with depth - years rather than an internship, and exposure to the
messy edges.
Score bands:
  0-2   No contact with logistics, freight, supply chain or trade operations
        anywhere in the record.
  3-4   Adjacency - sold to, built for, or integrated with logistics. An
        internship or a single project does not clear 4.
  5-6   One to two years in an operations-adjacent role touching shipments,
        planning or carrier coordination.
  7-8   Multiple years directly accountable inside a logistics operation,
        naming the messy edges - customs holds, carrier disputes, EDI or
        ICEGATE filings, port or terminal timelines.
  9-10  The above across more than one organisation or mode (sea and air,
        forwarder and terminal, shipper and 3PL), with regulatory or inspection
        exposure.
Source: Sunita's 7 years across documentation, DGFT, ICEGATE EDI and customs
audit; Rohan's CHA years at Nhava Sheva; Lavanya's carrier allocation at
Mahindra.

Criterion name: Documented Reversal or Failure
Weight: 20%
What a strong candidate looks like: Names a decision of their own that was
wrong or a system of theirs that broke, the root cause, and the change made -
and at senior level, evidence the lesson became someone else's practice.
Score bands:
  0-2   An unbroken record. Nothing ever stopped, lost or undone.
  3-4   Failure named but attributed to others or to inherited systems.
  5-6   Owns a problem inside their scope but not the decision behind it.
  7-8   Names their own wrong decision or broken system, the root cause, and
        the change they made in response.
  9-10  The above, and the lesson became someone else's practice - a standard,
        a checklist, a gate the team now runs.
Source: Aditya's lost-deal post-mortem that became standard practice before
account qualification; Lavanya's two killed features and owned outage
follow-ups.

Criterion name: Built the Missing Thing
Weight: 15%
What a strong candidate looks like: Established a standard, framework or
integration that outlived their involvement and that others adopted.
Score bands:
  0-2   Nothing created.
  3-4   Templates, documentation or improvements inside a process that already
        had an owner.
  5-6   Built something new that stayed within their own team.
  7-8   Established a standard, framework or integration adopted beyond their
        team.
  9-10  The above, and it outlived their involvement - still in use after they
        moved on, or absorbed into how the organisation works.
Source: Lavanya wrote the product's first formal API documentation and the
Tableau dashboard adopted by two other regional teams; Sunita's SOPs adopted in
full in 60 days; Aditya's case study programme now used by the full sales team.

Criterion name: Absorbed Load Without Escalation
Weight: 15%
What a strong candidate looks like: Held the line through a surge, a vendor
breaking on them, an inspection or an outage, and resolved it at their own
level.
Score bands:
  0-2   No evidence of operating under pressure.
  3-4   General claims of a fast-paced or high-volume environment, with nothing
        named.
  5-6   Handled more scope, with more resource.
  7-8   Held a surge, a vendor failure, an inspection or an outage at their own
        level and resolved it there.
  9-10  The above, sustained over months with the outcome held, and the
        response institutionalised.
Source: Sunita's weekend rebuild after the FMS vendor changed format without
notice, and two customs inspections closed without penalty; Rohan's 18-month
surge absorbed through process redesign.

SPM TOTAL: 100%
